Simon's Algorithm and Quantum Speedups

15-455 Complexity Theory Project

This website will introduce the basics of quantum computation and explore how Simon's algorithm achieves an exponential quantum query speedup over classical randomized algorithms.

Outline

  1. Introduction to Quantum Computation — Motivation for quantum speedups and query complexity.
  2. Quantum Computing Basics — Qubits, superposition, measurement, and gates.
  3. Quantum Circuits and Oracles — Circuit model and oracle queries.
  4. Simon's Problem — The hidden-string problem and its promise.
  5. Simon's Algorithm — The quantum procedure and linear equations.
  6. Quantum Correctness Proof — Why the algorithm works with O(n) queries.
  7. Classical Randomized Lower Bound — Collision probability and the exponential separation.
  8. Implications and Limitations — What the speedup does and does not show.

Sections

1. Introduction to Quantum Computation

I will introduce quantum computation and explain why it offers a different approach to solving certain problems. I will also motivate the study of quantum speedups by defining query complexity and comparing quantum and classical models.

2. Quantum Computing Basics

I will review the core ideas needed to understand quantum algorithms, including qubits, superposition, interference, and measurement. I will also explain how common quantum gates transform states and how multiple qubits are represented together.

3. Quantum Circuits and Oracles

I will describe the quantum circuit model and show how a sequence of gates carries out a computation. I will then explain quantum oracles, how they encode black-box functions, and how oracle calls are counted when analyzing query complexity.

4. Simon's Problem

I will formally define Simon's problem, including the promise that a function hides a nonzero bit string through a two-to-one mapping. I will illustrate the goal of recovering that hidden string and explain why the promise is essential to the problem.

5. Simon's Algorithm

I will walk through Simon's quantum algorithm step by step, from preparing a superposition to querying the oracle and measuring the registers. I will show how repeated runs produce linear equations that can be solved to recover the hidden string.

6. Quantum Correctness Proof

I will prove that each measurement produces a uniformly random bit string orthogonal to the hidden string. I will then show that enough independent equations are obtained with high probability, allowing the algorithm to succeed using only O(n) oracle queries.

7. Classical Randomized Lower Bound

I will analyze why a classical randomized algorithm must find a collision among the oracle's outputs to learn the hidden string. Using the birthday-paradox argument, I will derive the exponential query lower bound and compare it with the quantum algorithm's linear query complexity.

8. Implications and Limitations

I will discuss what Simon's algorithm demonstrates about exponential quantum speedups and its influence on later algorithms such as Shor's. I will also clarify the result's limitations, including its reliance on a promise problem, an ideal oracle, and query complexity rather than total real-world runtime.

References to explore: MIT Quantum Complexity Theory lecture notes; IBM Quantum's Simon's algorithm tutorial.
