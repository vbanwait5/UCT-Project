We prove two claims: each sample is uniform on s⊥, and O(n) samples span that subspace with high probability.

Proof chapter25–30 minute readIncludes rank lab

1. The exact output distribution

Lemma 1 (Fourier sampling a Simon coset)

For any x0 and nonzero s, applying H⊗n to (|x0⟩ + |x0 ⊕ s⟩)/√2 and measuring yields a uniformly random z ∈ s⊥.

Proof. The amplitude of |z⟩ is

αz = (−1)x₀·z[1 + (−1)s·z] / √(2n+1).

When s·z=1, αz=0. When s·z=0, |αz| = 2/√(2n+1) = 1/√(2n−1). The subspace s⊥ has dimension n−1 and therefore contains 2n−1 vectors. Each has probability 1/2n−1, and the probabilities sum to one. ∎

The distribution is independent of x0. This is why measuring, discarding, or never explicitly naming the second-register outcome all lead to the same final sample distribution.

2. When do the equations determine s?

Let d=n−1. After choosing a basis for s⊥, the sampled rows are independent uniform vectors in 𝔽2d. If the current rows span an r-dimensional subspace, the next row is dependent with probability 2r/2d.

Proposition 2 (Full-rank probability)

Exactly d samples are linearly independent with probability

∏r=0d−1(1 − 2r−d) = ∏j=1d(1 − 2−j).

This product approaches approximately 0.288 as d grows. So n−1 samples already succeed with constant probability, but a few extra samples give a much cleaner high-probability guarantee.

3. A high-probability bound

Theorem 3 (Rank accumulation)

Let z1,…,zt be independent uniform samples from s⊥. Then

Pr[span{z1,…,zt} ≠ s⊥] < 2(n−1)−t.

Proof. Work in the d-dimensional space s⊥. If the rows fail to span, there exists a nonzero vector w orthogonal to every sampled row. For fixed nonzero w, a uniform row is orthogonal to w with probability 1/2, so all t rows are orthogonal with probability 2−t. Union bound over the 2d−1 nonzero choices of w. ∎

Taking t = n−1+c makes failure probability less than 2−c. Thus O(n + log(1/δ)) queries suffice for failure probability at most δ.

Connection: amplification in probabilistic time

As with BPP error reduction, independent repetition converts constant success into exponentially small failure. Here the aggregation rule is not majority vote: it is rank growth in a vector space.

The query complexity is linear, but the full idealized algorithm also includes polynomial classical work. This distinction is central: an exponential query separation is stronger than an algorithmic anecdote, but weaker than a hardware runtime benchmark.

6. Proof subtleties worth noticing

  • Zero is a valid sample. It gives the tautology 0·s=0 and adds no rank.
  • Samples are uniform only under the exact promise. Noise or extra collisions change the state and its Fourier spectrum.
  • Rank n−1 is the stopping condition. Rank n is impossible because every row lies in s⊥.
  • The algorithm is Las Vegas with verification. Under the promise, once a nonzero candidate s′ is found, two additional queries can check f(0)=f(s′); if verification fails, continue sampling.

Takeaway

Correctness decomposes cleanly into a quantum lemma (uniform Fourier sampling of s⊥) and a classical lemma (random rows span an (n−1)-dimensional space after O(n) samples).
